minitart
Security GitHub

GitHub Actions runners on your own Mac mini.

minitart runs every job in a fresh virtual machine and deletes it when the job ends. A job reaches the internet and nothing of yours: not your files, not your keychain, not your home network.

Set it up Read the code

Works best on one Mac with a handful of active private repos.

In your menu bar

One command installs minitart and a menu bar app for it, together:

brew install --cask zurfyx/tap/minitart-app

Its mark says how things are: a prompt when idle, the number of jobs running, an exclamation mark when minitart needs you, and a pause sign while it drains. Its menu lists the jobs and opens the dashboard.

How it works

  1. A workflow asks for it

    runs-on: minitart-linux or minitart-macos. Every other job stays where it was.

  2. The job gets a new VM

    minitart clones an image in under a second, boots it and registers a runner that exists for that one job.

  3. The VM is deleted

    When the job ends, so does everything it wrote, installed or left running. The next job starts from the image again.

What keeps it safe

The same isolation hosted runners have
One VM per job, on Apple's hypervisor, driven by Tart. No folder is shared with a VM.
The network is an allowlist
A VM can send to public internet addresses and nowhere else. Your router, your NAS and your tailnet are out of reach without being listed, and so is the next thing you add.
You choose the repos twice
minitart is a GitHub App you install on repos, and it cannot register a runner anywhere else. A repo is served once you also turn it on in the dashboard, so installing the App on your whole account by mistake serves nothing.
A job never sees a key
The App's key stays on the Mac. A VM holds the single-use config of its own runner, already spent.
You can check
minitart selftest boots a VM and tries to reach your router and your Mac from inside it.
Small enough to read
A manager and a page. TypeScript that Node runs as it is, with no runtime dependencies.

What it does not cover is written down too: the security model.

What it is not

It is not for a team with a fleet of Macs. It does not fall back to hosted runners when the Mac is off, it runs arm64 only, and it keeps no caches between jobs. Switching a job back to GitHub's runners is a one-word change.
